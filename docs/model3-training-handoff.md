Different hand cards can therefore produce indistinguishable candidate vectors. `train_second.py` trains the same small architecture on ordinary-action imitation labels; whole-game validation agreement measures teacher imitation, not win strength. Increasing updates alone cannot recover distinctions absent from these inputs.

## Evolution and Phantom Dive audit

The retained action journal has zero selected evolution actions and zero Phantom Dive attacks across twelve Model2 intents. It does **not** retain each decision's hand, candidate set or scores. Counts of legal evolution offers, turns with Drakloak in hand, or model decisions declining a legal evolution are therefore unknown, not zero. Do not label this run as proof that Model2 ignored available evolutions.

The live mapping includes evolution explicitly: it checks `MatchUserOperations.CanEvolveCard(source, target, "", null, None)` for visible own cards against own active/bench targets. A true result creates a normal `play` candidate with source/target IDs and an `EvolveCard` dispatch mapping. This matches the source signature and normal native legality gate. No live evolution was selected to demonstrate execution of that route, and no eligibility counters were saved. A mapping fault is neither established nor fully excluded by the pilot.

There is a concrete upstream helper bias. `chooseEntityPrompt` ranks Lillie's Determination 10, Dreepy 9, Drakloak 8 and Dragapult ex 7. The retained searches selected Lillie's Determination through Meowth, Dreepy plus Dunsparce through Poffin, and Dreepy through Poké Pad. The last is a helper decision, not a network choice. The helper would prefer Dreepy to Drakloak if both were offered; the actual alternative list was not retained, so that counterfactual remains unverified. Poffin's basic-only search should not be counted as a missed Drakloak opportunity.

A read-only post-stop screenshot showed an empty active slot awaiting promotion, a bench of Budew, Munkidori, Meowth ex, Dreepy and Dunsparce, and Dragapult ex in hand. No Drakloak was visible in hand or play. The stage-2 card therefore had no visible stage-1 target in that snapshot. The image is private and was not published. This snapshot was taken after the user stopped the controller; it cannot reconstruct the earlier draws, search alternatives or pre-attack legality. No adapter was attached and no input was sent for this audit.

The training change must address both the ordinary scorer and search decisions. Learn evolution-chain development, card search and Energy allocation from player-visible trajectories and game outcomes; do not hardcode "evolve whenever possible" or force Phantom Dive. Add diagnostics for each policy decision: legal evolve count, stage/source/target relations, eligible evolution targets, hand stage availability, chosen action and score/rank gaps. Keep private identities outside published logs. During held-out evaluation report time to first effective Phantom Dive, successful evolution conversions per legal opportunity, useful damage/knockouts and overall outcomes. These measure learned development without prescribing a fixed move sequence. Existing helper decisions must remain attributed until a learned prompt policy replaces them.
